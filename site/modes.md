# Game modes and settings

Frozen Bubble has more to it than the 100-level campaign. This page lists
every way to play, alone or with others, and the settings that change how the
game looks and feels. The rules themselves are on the
[how to play](../how-to-play/) page.

![The title menu](../screenshots/title-menu.jpg)

## 1-player modes

All of these are in the 1-player menu, the first button on the title screen.

### Play the default levelset

The classic campaign: the original game's 100 hand-made levels, from level 1.
The early levels teach the basics with two or three colours; the later ones
use all eight and are built around one or two shots that bring the whole
board down. When you lose a life the game offers **Continue**, which retries
the level you died on, or **Start over**, which goes back to level 1.
Continuing lets you see the rest of the levels, but the run stops counting
for the high score tables from that point. Only a campaign run started at
level 1 can reach the [online highscores](../scores/).

### Pick levelset and level

Start any levelset at any level you have already reached. Useful for practising
a level that keeps beating you without replaying the ones before it.

### Random levels

An endless series of freshly generated boards. There is no last level, so the
only question is how far you get. It is the best way to learn to read a new
layout quickly, since you never see the same board twice.

### Multiplayer training

You play alone against the clock with attack bubbles on, and your score is
made of the attacks you would have sent. It is good practice for online
play, where the hurry timer is shorter and big drops matter more than
individual pops.

## Multiplayer modes

Multiplayer rounds can be played on one computer by two to five players, or
online with up to twenty. The host picks one of four modes:

| Mode | How a round is won |
| --- | --- |
| **Classic** | The original game. Your board loses when a bubble crosses the bottom line; the last player or team alive wins. |
| **Clear** | The first player to empty their own board wins. New rows and attacks are switched off, since nobody is being pushed down. |
| **Race** | The first to pop a target number of bubbles wins: anywhere from 10 to 200, 50 by default. Losing your board still knocks you out. |
| **Timed** | The most bubbles popped when time runs out wins: from 15 seconds to five minutes, 30 seconds by default. An exact tie at the top is a draw. |

Any mode can be played in **teams** of up to five sides. Teammates share a
round win, attacks never hit your own team, and in Timed mode a team's pops
are added together. The host also chooses how many round wins take the match,
the attack setting (On, Blockable or Off), whether chain reactions are on, and,
per player, the number of colours, how often new rows drop and whether the aim
guide is shown. The [multiplayer guide](../multiplayer/) explains each of
these.

**Tournaments** are a separate online format: single-elimination brackets for
four to sixteen players, each match best of three, with one ruleset chosen by
the organiser for every match.

## Settings

Most settings are under **CONTROLS & SETTINGS** on the title screen; the
1-player options are in the 1-player menu itself.

### Controls

Every player's keys can be changed, including Swap, and any gamepad button
can be assigned. **Mouse / touch aim** lets you aim by pointing at the board
and fire by clicking or lifting your finger. Controls can also be changed in
the middle of a game from the in-game menu.

### Speed

The game speed can be set from 1x to 5x. Higher speeds make bubbles fly
faster and everything happen sooner, which is harder but also saves time on
the long levels.

### Bubbles and graphics

- **Bubbles**: *Candy* (new, glossy bubbles, the default) or *Classic*
  (the original artwork).
- **Colour-blind bubbles**: a set whose bubbles differ in pattern as well as
  colour, so telling them apart does not rely on hue alone.
- **Menu style**: six looks for the title screen, from *Classic* to *Modern*.
- **Graphics quality**: *Low* turns off some effects for slower devices.
- **Aim guide** (1-player): draws the line your shot will take. Runs with it on
  do not count for the high score tables.

### Sound

Sound effects and music can be switched separately from the in-game menu.

### High scores and accounts

**Online highscores** in the 1-player menu decides whether your campaign runs
are sent to the world board. **Account code** shows the code that keeps your
scores together across devices. Both are described in the
[high scores FAQ](../faq/).

## Replays

Finished rounds are saved as replays, and the **Replays** page in
CONTROLS & SETTINGS plays them back, deletes them, and exports or imports them
so you can share a good game.
