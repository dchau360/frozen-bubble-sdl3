# Frozen Bubble strategy guide

Anyone can pop three bubbles of the same colour. Getting through all 100
levels, or beating a room of other players, comes down to a handful of habits
that experienced players use on almost every shot. This guide collects them,
roughly in the order they are worth learning. If you are new to the game,
start with [how to play](../how-to-play/).

## Think in drops, not pops

A popped bubble is worth 10 points; a dropped one is worth 20, and in a
multiplayer game every dropped bubble also counts toward the attack you send.
More importantly, a drop can remove dozens of bubbles in one shot, while a pop
removes three or four. So the first question on every shot is not "where does
this colour match?" but "which match, if I popped it, would leave the most
bubbles hanging from nothing?"

Look for narrow necks: places where a large cluster hangs from the ceiling, or
from the rest of the board, through only one or two bubbles. Pop the neck and
everything below it falls. Levels are often designed around one such point, and
finding it early can turn a hard level into two shots.

## Use the walls

The launcher only fires in a straight line, but the side walls bounce bubbles
cleanly, and a bank shot reaches places a direct shot cannot: behind an
overhang, into the side of a group, or to the far corner when the middle is
blocked. A few things to remember:

- The angle out equals the angle in, so a shot that hits the wall halfway up
  lands about as far across again on the other side.
- Shallow angles bounce many times and are hard to judge. Steeper shots with a
  single bounce are far more reliable.
- A bubble sticks to the first bubble it touches, including a glancing touch
  on the way past. Leave a little room either side of the path you picture.

If you are still learning the angles, the **Aim guide** option draws the line
your shot will follow, bounces included. Runs played with it on do not count
for the high score tables, so treat it as practice.

## Plan two colours ahead

You always know the next bubble, and with Swap you can hold a third. Before you
fire, check what is coming: if the loaded bubble has nowhere useful to go but
the next one would set up a big drop, put the loaded one in the pocket and take
the drop now. If the loaded bubble is the colour you need in a few shots'
time, park it in the pocket and keep it there until the spot opens up.

When no shot pops anything, place the bubble where it builds toward a future
pop instead of wherever is closest. Two of a colour sitting next to each other
are one shot away from popping; a lone bubble of an unwanted colour buried in
another group is a problem you will have to dig out later.

## Clear colours completely

The game only deals colours that are still on the board. Once you clear the
last bubble of a colour, it stops coming, and every shot after that has fewer
possible colours and a better chance of matching. Late in a level it is often
worth spending a shot or two to finish off the last two or three bubbles of a
rare colour rather than chasing a bigger pop elsewhere.

## Watch the ceiling and the clock

In the 1-player game the ceiling comes down one row every nine shots. That
puts a price on every shot that does not pop anything. Keep an eye on the row
of lights that counts down to the next drop and try to time your big clears
so the board is shallow when the ceiling moves.

The hurry timer is the other limit. Thinking is fine, but if you take too long
the game fires for you, often somewhere useless. On a hard board it is better
to make a reasonable shot early than a perfect one late.

## Fast clears pay

Clearing a 1-player level within five seconds is worth a 5,000-point bonus,
falling by 84 points for every second after that. On the points board that
bonus usually matters more than the bubbles themselves, so a player chasing a
high score plays quickly through the easy levels and saves the careful thinking
for the hard ones.

## Multiplayer habits

Against other players the same skills apply, with a few more on top:

- **Big drops attack.** Every shot sends your opponents attack bubbles equal to
  the bubbles you popped plus the ones you dropped, minus two. One large drop
  can bury an opponent who is close to the line.
- **Mind the new rows.** In multiplayer a new row is added to the top of every
  board after a set number of shots, twelve by default, so a board that is
  already deep gets dangerous fast.
- **Blockable attacks.** In rooms set to *Blockable*, your own pops first pay
  off attacks still queued against you. When a big wave is coming, a quick pop
  can cancel it before it lands.
- **Pick your target.** In bigger rooms you can aim your attacks at one
  opponent. Finishing off a player who is nearly out is often better than
  spreading damage around.

The [multiplayer guide](../multiplayer/) covers these rules in detail.

## Practise on purpose

Two modes exist mostly for practice. **Random levels** deals an endless series
of new boards, good for learning to read a layout quickly. **Multiplayer
training** puts you against the clock with attack bubbles on, scoring the
attacks you would have sent, which is the best way to get used to multiplayer
pace without an opponent. Both are in the 1-player menu.
